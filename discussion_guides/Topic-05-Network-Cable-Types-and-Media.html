Five network media choices

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A transmission medium carries signals between network interfaces. Twisted-pair copper, fiber, coaxial cable, and radio differ in construction, reach, interference behavior, and installation needs.

  • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP): Four unshielded twisted copper pairs, no overall shield. Twisting reduces electromagnetic interference (EMI).
  • Shielded Twisted Pair (STP): Shielded twisted-pair cable with foil or braid shielding; requires proper grounding and bonding.
  • Fiber: Carries light signals through glass or plastic cores; immune to EMI and spans long distances.
  • Coaxial: Copper core with dielectric insulator and braided metallic shield; used for broadband and legacy bus.
  • Wireless: Radio frequency transmission providing mobility across shared, unguided electromagnetic airspace.

Three Ethernet cable categories

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A cable category describes specified performance characteristics for compatible Ethernet installations. Supported rate and reach also depend on the standard, connectors, channel length, and installation quality.

  • Cat 5e: Supports 1 Gb/s (1000BASE-T) over a standard 100 m channel.
  • Cat 6: Supports 1 Gb/s at 100 m; supports 10 Gb/s (10GBASE-T) up to 55 m.
  • Cat 6A: Supports 10 Gb/s (10GBASE-T) over a full 100 m channel.

RJ45 Plugs and Keystone Jacks

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Twisted-pair Ethernet uses Eight Positions, Eight Contacts (8P8C) modular plugs for patch cords and 110 Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) keystone jacks for permanent wall runs. Both components must match the cable category and wiring standard.

  • Registered Jack 45 (RJ45) (Eight Positions, Eight Contacts (8P8C)) plug: Modular male plug with eight gold-plated contacts, used to terminate flexible stranded patch cables.
  • Keystone Jack: Modular female jack with 110 Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) contacts, mounted in wall faceplates and patch panels for permanent solid-core horizontal cabling.
  • Category matching: Plugs and jacks must match the cable category and conductor gauge to prevent signal degradation.

T568A: eight conductor positions

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

T568A specifies which conductor occupies each of the eight contact positions. Read the color order from pin 1 to pin 8 using the stated connector orientation.

  • Pin 1: White/Green.
  • Pin 2: Green.
  • Pin 3: White/Orange.
  • Pin 4: Blue.
  • Pin 5: White/Blue.
  • Pin 6: Orange.
  • Pin 7: White/Brown.
  • Pin 8: Brown.

Straight-Through vs Crossover Cables

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A straight-through cable uses the same termination standard at both ends. A traditional crossover changes selected pair positions, although many modern interfaces can adjust automatically.

  • A conventional A–B crossover swaps pins 1↔3 and 2↔6 for traditional 10/100BASE-T transmit/receive pairs.
  • Many modern interfaces support auto-MDI/Medium Dependent Interface Crossover (MDIX), so a crossover cable is often unnecessary. Gigabit Ethernet uses all four pairs.
  • An equal but nonstandard color order may pass a simple continuity check while creating split pairs and poor performance.

Four cabling tool and tester functions

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Cabling tools prepare, terminate, or test different parts of an installation. Stripping, crimping, punch-down termination, and continuity testing each require the appropriate tool.

  • Cable stripper: removes jacket without nicking conductor insulation.
  • Crimping tool: terminates compatible modular plugs.
  • Punch-down tool: seats conductors in compatible Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) jacks or patch panels.
  • Basic Local Area Network (LAN) tester: checks conductor continuity and wire mapping.

RJ45 Cable Crimping Tools

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Terminating an Eight Positions, Eight Contacts (8P8C) modular plug requires preparing dedicated cutting, stripping, crimping, and testing tools alongside quality Cat 5e/Cat 6 media.

  • Registered Jack 45 (RJ45) Modular Crimper: Heavy-duty ratcheting tool with precision Eight Positions, Eight Contacts (8P8C) dies to compress contacts into copper conductors and engage the jacket strain relief.
  • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) Cable Stripper: Compact rotary wire stripper calibrated to cleanly score and remove the outer PVC jacket without nicking conductor insulation.
  • Modular Registered Jack 45 (RJ45) Plugs: Cat 5e or Cat 6 clear polycarbonate male Eight Positions, Eight Contacts (8P8C) plugs featuring eight gold-plated contact pins and a flexible locking tab.
  • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) Cable: Four-pair 24 AWG stranded copper Ethernet cable designed for flexible patch cords between workstations and wall jacks.
  • Flush Cutters / Snips: Precision diagonal cutting pliers used to trim untwisted, aligned wires straight and square across before insertion into the plug.
  • Local Area Network (LAN) Cable Tester: Dual-unit continuity wire-map tester to confirm all eight pin paths and detect open circuits, short circuits, or crossed pairs.

Keystone Jack Punch-Down Tools

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Terminating a permanent wall jack requires specialized insulation displacement tools, punch-down supports, and rated solid-core cabling.

  • 110 Impact Punch-Down Tool: Spring-loaded impact driver with a reversible 110/66 blade that seats conductors into Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) V-notches and cuts excess wire flush.
  • Keystone Jack (110 Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC)): Registered Jack 45 (RJ45) female modular jack with 110 insulation displacement contacts, universal T568A/T568B color coding, and snap-in faceplate clips.
  • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) Cable Stripper: Round jacket stripper used to remove 1 to 1.5 inches of outer jacket while preserving pair twists up to the Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) blocks.
  • Solid-Core Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) Cable: Four-pair 23/24 AWG solid copper horizontal cable engineered for structured in-wall and ceiling cable runs.
  • Punch-Down Palm Tool / Stand: Contoured palm puck or base holder that cradles the jack securely to prevent hand injury and ensure even downward punching pressure.
  • Insulation-Displacement Contact (IDC) Dust Caps & Cable Tester: Snap-on protective dust covers to lock conductors firmly in place, plus a Local Area Network (LAN) tester with patch cord to verify end-to-end pin mapping.

Three common wire-map faults

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A wire map identifies which contact paths connect between cable ends. Opens, shorts, and miswires describe different continuity problems and require different corrections.

  • Open: a conductor has no continuous end-to-end path.
  • Short: conductors are accidentally joined.
  • Miswire: a conductor arrives at an unexpected pin.
💡 Brief Explanation After Reading (Teacher's Discussion Notes)

🌟 Real-World Analogy / Intuition

Compare network cable types, categories, wiring standards, termination, and wire mapping.

🗣️ How to Explain It (Talking Points)

  • Open: a conductor has no continuous end-to-end path.
  • Short: conductors are accidentally joined.
  • Miswire: a conductor arrives at an unexpected pin.
  • A wire-map pass does not certify pair integrity, signal quality, or cable bandwidth.
