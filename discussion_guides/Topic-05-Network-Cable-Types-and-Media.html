Five network media choices

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A transmission medium carries signals between network interfaces. Twisted-pair copper, fiber, coaxial cable, and radio differ in construction, reach, interference behavior, and installation needs.

  • Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP): Four unshielded twisted copper pairs, no overall shield. Twisting reduces electromagnetic interference (EMI).
  • Shielded Twisted Pair (STP): Shielded twisted-pair cable with foil or braid shielding; requires proper grounding and bonding.
  • Fiber: Carries light signals through glass or plastic cores; immune to EMI and spans long distances.
  • Coaxial: Copper core with dielectric insulator and braided metallic shield; used for broadband and legacy bus.
  • Wireless: Radio frequency transmission providing mobility across shared, unguided electromagnetic airspace.

Three Ethernet cable categories

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A cable category describes specified performance characteristics for compatible Ethernet installations. Supported rate and reach also depend on the standard, connectors, channel length, and installation quality.

  • Cat 5e: Supports 1 Gb/s (1000BASE-T) over a standard 100 m channel.
  • Cat 6: Supports 1 Gb/s at 100 m; supports 10 Gb/s (10GBASE-T) up to 55 m.
  • Cat 6A: Supports 10 Gb/s (10GBASE-T) over a full 100 m channel.

Fiber-Optic Cable Construction and Types

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Optical fiber carries light pulses through glass or plastic cores instead of electrical signals. Single-mode and multi-mode fiber provide immune, high-speed, long-distance communication.

  • Core and Cladding: Ultra-thin glass core surrounded by reflective cladding that bounces light along the fiber using total internal reflection.
  • Single-Mode Fiber (SMF): Extremely thin 9-micron core using laser light; spans dozens of kilometers for campus backbones and telco WANs.
  • Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF): Wider 50 or 62.5-micron core using LED or VCSEL light; spans up to 550 m for high-speed data center links.
  • Immunity to Interference: Because light does not conduct electricity, fiber is immune to electrical noise, motors, and lightning surges.

Coaxial Cable Construction and Shielding

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Coaxial cable uses a concentric four-layer design that provides physical durability and electrical shielding. It is widely used in cable broadband modems and legacy bus architectures.

  • Center Conductor: Solid copper or copper-clad steel wire that carries the high-frequency radio frequency signal.
  • Dielectric Insulator: Non-conductive plastic foam layer that maintains precise physical spacing between center conductor and shield.
  • Braided Metallic Shield: Woven copper or aluminum foil that blocks external electrical interference and completes the circuit ground.
  • Common Use Cases: Modern cable broadband modems (DOCSIS), closed-circuit TV (CCTV), and legacy 10BASE2/10BASE5 bus networks.

Unguided Media: Wireless Radio Frequencies

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Unguided media transmit electromagnetic radio waves through the open air without physical cables. Wireless provides endpoint mobility across shared radio spectrum.

  • Radio Frequencies (RF): Transmits digital data modulated onto microwave and radio carrier frequencies between 2.4 GHz and 6 GHz.
  • Shared Transmission Airspace: Unlike dedicated copper links, all wireless stations in an area share the same radio frequency spectrum.
  • Environmental Attenuation: Radio waves are absorbed, reflected, and scattered by concrete walls, metal filing cabinets, and water.
  • Convenience vs. Stability: Wireless enables laptop and smartphone mobility, while wired Ethernet delivers guaranteed speed and zero interference.

Campus Inter-Building Media Selection

application
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A standard 100 m copper Ethernet channel cannot span outdoor campus runs or withstand ground potential differences. Optical fiber provides electrical isolation and multi-kilometer reach.

  • A standard 100 m copper Ethernet channel is insufficient for this direct path.
  • A suitable fiber link with appropriate transceivers can meet reach and isolation requirements.
  • Installation needs rated materials and professional planning; fiber is not simply a longer Registered Jack 45 (RJ45) cable.

Campus Media Selection Walkthrough

walkthrough
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Plan campus transmission links by evaluating channel distance, interference conditions, and transceiver requirements. Always verify physical cable ratings before deployment.

  • Identify the required channel distance: horizontal floor runs under 90 m can use Category 6 twisted pair, while outdoor building runs over 100 m require optical fiber.
  • Compare interference conditions: factory floors or conduits near high-voltage lines require fiber or properly bonded Shielded Twisted Pair (STP) cable.
  • Select optical fiber profiles: single-mode fiber for long-distance inter-building backbones, and multi-mode fiber for high-speed switch-to-switch links within a data center.
  • Verify physical termination and transceiver compatibility before applying power or configuring network interfaces.
