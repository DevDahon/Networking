Three gateway interface and translation concepts

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A combined gateway connects local clients to another network through separate interface roles. Address translation can change packet addresses, but it does not replace routing or access-control policy.

  • Local Area Network (LAN) interface: faces local clients.
  • Wide Area Network (WAN) interface: faces another network, usually the provider. Working Internet service also needs provider connectivity and routes.
  • Network Address Translation (NAT): translates addresses; a gateway commonly uses port translation so private clients can share a public Internet Protocol version 4 (IPv4) address.
💡 Brief Explanation After Reading (Teacher's Discussion Notes)

🌟 Real-World Analogy / Intuition

Staff PCs and visitor phones share one provider connection, but visitors should not browse staff folders.

🗣️ How to Explain It (Talking Points)

  • LAN interface: faces local clients.
  • WAN interface: faces another network, usually the provider.
  • Working Internet service also needs provider connectivity and routes.
  • NAT: translates addresses; a gateway commonly uses port translation so private clients can share a public IPv4 address.
  • NAT does not replace firewall policy.
  • Local connectivity does not prove WAN or DNS service.

SSID, Authentication, and Isolation

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A Service Set Identifier names a wireless network, while authentication controls who can join it. Guest isolation is a separate policy controlling access to private resources.

  • Wireless security: avoid open networks, Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP), and obsolete Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA)/Temporal Key Integrity Protocol (TKIP). Choose a supported protected configuration.
  • Choose a long unique passphrase and protect router administrator credentials separately. Hiding an Service Set Identifier (SSID) is not a security control.
  • A guest network should be intentionally isolated from private Local Area Network (LAN) resources. Check the real product’s isolation behavior.

Two protected Wi-Fi security options

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Protected wireless access needs a security mode supported by both access point and clients. Modern personal-network options use suitable authentication and encryption rather than relying on a hidden network name.

  • WPA3-Personal: use when supported by the router and clients.
  • WPA2-Personal with Advanced Encryption Standard (AES): use for compatible devices that need this protected configuration.

Wi-Fi Frequencies and Standards

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Wireless networks operate over unlicensed radio frequencies using standardized IEEE 802.11 protocols that balance speed and range.

  • 2.4 GHz Frequency Band: Travels farther and penetrates walls well, but offers lower speeds and suffers high congestion from Bluetooth and microwaves.
  • 5 GHz Frequency Band: Delivers much faster data speeds and less interference, but has a shorter range and struggles to pass through solid walls.
  • Wi-Fi 4 (802.11n): Introduced dual-band operation and MIMO antenna streams, reaching up to 600 Mbps.
  • Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac): High-speed 5 GHz standard providing gigabit wireless speeds for modern laptops and mobile devices.
  • Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax): Modern standard operating on 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz (Wi-Fi 6E), optimized for crowded environments like schools and stadiums.

Coordinating Router, Pool, and Wi-Fi

concept
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Router settings affect both management access and client connectivity. A compatible local address, allocation pool, and wireless configuration reduce avoidable disconnections.

  • Choose a private Local Area Network (LAN) subnet, then a pool of usable addresses inside it excluding the router’s address.
  • Changing the router Local Area Network (LAN) address can interrupt management access; reconnect and obtain a lease in the new subnet.
  • Save settings, connect one wired and one wireless client, then verify Internet Protocol (IP) options, gateway reachability, and service access separately.

Applying and Testing Router Settings

walkthrough
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

A router change must be followed by reconnecting clients and checking their received settings. Local access, name resolution, and upstream service provide different verification evidence.

  • Use an isolated, authorized router and record its current settings. A wired management connection reduces dependence on changing Wi-Fi settings.
  • Choose a private Local Area Network (LAN) subnet and a matching pool excluding the router address. A LAN address change can interrupt management and existing leases.
  • Set an Service Set Identifier (SSID) and supported WPA3-Personal or WPA2-AES security. Use a long unique passphrase distinct from router administrator credentials.
  • Reconnect a wired and an authenticated wireless client, then inspect assigned address, mask, gateway, and Domain Name System (DNS).
  • Check local access before Wide Area Network (WAN) services. Guest isolation depends on the actual device features and policy; restore approved settings after a physical demonstration.

A small training center

application
📖 What to Read & Display on Screen

Staff and visitors may share an Internet connection without sharing access to internal files. A correctly configured guest network separates those access needs.

  • Use a private staff Local Area Network (LAN) and a properly isolated guest Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) supported by the equipment.
  • Document Wide Area Network (WAN) requirements from the provider instead of guessing settings.
  • Confirm guests cannot access staff resources while approved Internet access remains possible.
