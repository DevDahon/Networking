How much data can it carry?
Almost everything can be measured in some way - How
tall are you? How old are you? How clever are you? and
so on.
Communication channels are just the same - there are
ways of describing their most important attributes.
One of those attributes is "How much data can it carry".
This measure is called "BANDWIDTH".

PAGE 3
What does bandwidth measure?
Bandwidth measures of the capacity of a communications
channel.
Digital Bandwidth is the maximum amount of data that can
travel along a communications channel in a given time.
The smallest piece of digital data is the 'bit' and time
interval is measured in seconds.
And so a sensible measure of data capacity is "bits per
second".

PAGE 4
Bandwidth and the Internet
Machines on the Internet are connected to each other 
through a network of some kind.
Most of the computers are connected 
by physical cables such as copper 
cable or perhaps optical fibre. 
However there are alternatives such 
as wireless, microwave or even laser.
No matter what the method used, it is the bandwidth of the 
communication channel that determines how much data travels over 
any given time.


PAGE 5
Bandwidth and the Internet
If your computer is connected to a remote server somewhere,
what determines the maximum speed of data transfer is the
part of the connection that has the lowest bandwidth - this
becomes the bottleneck.
Quite often the bottleneck is the "last mile" between your
house and the local telephone exchange. This is because the
connection is making use of plain old telephone wires that
were originally designed to only carry voice signals.
Therefore 'bandwidth' determines the overall performance
limit of a network

PAGE 6
Bandwidth Speed
Modern internet links are measured in Kilobits per second (Kbps) i.e
thousands of bits per second. Or, in Megabits (Mbps) - Millions of bits per
second.
These are the bandwidths of some connections to the Internet:
• 300 - 9600 bits per second : Early modems
• 56 kilobits per second: Bandwidth of dial-up modem (some people in
remote areas still have to use these)
• 64 - 128Kbps: ISDN, obsolete, replaced by ADSL broadband
• 512kbps - 20Mbps: Bandwidth of most home ADSL internet links
• 20Mbps - 100Mbps: Superfast broadband

PAGE 7
Throughput
• Throughput is the quantity of error free data
transmitted per unit of time
• Measured in Bps, or Packets Per Second(PPS)

PAGE 8
Factors that affect throughput
• The size of packets
• Inter-frame gaps between packets
• Packets-per-second ratings of devices that forward packets
• Client speed(CPU, Memory, and HD Access Speed)
• Server speed(CPU, Memory, and HD Access Speed)
• Network design
• Protocols
• Distance
• Errors 
• Time of Day
