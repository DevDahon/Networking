What is Computer Networking?
A computer network is a system of interconnected computing devices—ranging from traditional to cloud-based environments—that communicate and share resources with one another.

Key Milestones in the History of Computer Networking
1960s – The Beginning
1980s – The Internet Emerges
2000s – Global Expansion
Networking started with ARPANET, a U.S. defense project that connected universities and research centers. It was the first real computer network.
1970s – Protocols Developed
1990s – World Wide Web




Researchers Vinton Cerf and Robert Kahn created TCP/IP protocols (Transmission Control Protocol / Internet Protocol). These became the standard rules for how computers communicate.
ARPANET officially adopted TCP/IP in 1983, marking the birth of the internet. Local Area Networks (LANs) also became common in schools and businesses.
Tim Berners-Lee introduced the World Wide Web (WWW) in 1991, making the internet accessible to the public with websites and browsers.
Wi-Fi, mobile data (3G/4G), and broadband spread worldwide. Networking became part of everyday life.

Types of Computer Networks
A PAN is the smallest type of network, typically used by a single person within a few meters. It connects personal devices like smartphones, laptops, and wearable tech using Bluetooth or USB. PANs are ideal for short-range communication and device syncing.

Examples:

Connecting a smartphone to wireless earbuds via Bluetooth

Syncing a smartwatch with a laptop


Personal Area Network (PAN)

ppt/slides/slide12.xml
Types of Computer Networks
A LAN covers a small geographic area such as a home, office, or school. It connects computers and devices within the same building using Ethernet cables or Wi-Fi. LANs are fast, secure, and commonly used for sharing files, printers, and internet access.

Examples:

School computer lab network

Office network connecting employee desktops and printers

Local Area Network (LAN)

Types of Computer Networks
A MAN spans a city or large campus, connecting multiple LANs within a metropolitan area. It’s larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN, often used by government agencies, universities, or large companies to link buildings or branches.

Examples:

City-wide public Wi-Fi network

Un iversity campus network connecting multiple buildings

Characteristics of Computer Networking
Networks must protect data from unauthorized access, hacking, and malware. Security features like firewalls, encryption, and authentication help safeguard sensitive information and ensure privacy.
1. Security
A reliable network ensures consistent availability of data and services. Backup systems and redundancy help maintain operations even during failures or disruptions.

2. Reliability

ppt/slides/slide16.xml
Characteristics of Computer Networking
Scalable networks can grow by adding more devices or users without losing performance. This is essential for expanding businesses, schools, or online platforms.
3. Scalability
Networking enables smooth data transfer between devices. Whether sharing files or accessing cloud services, networks ensure that information moves efficiently.
4. Flow of Data

Characteristics of Computer Networking
Performance refers to speed and responsiveness. Fast data transfer, low latency, and high bandwidth improve user experience and support real-time communication.
5. High Performance
Fault-tolerant networks continue to operate even when part of the system fails. Alternate paths or backup connections ensure uninterrupted communication.
6. Fault Tolerance
