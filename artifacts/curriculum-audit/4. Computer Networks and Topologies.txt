Introduction
• A computer network is an interconnection of two or
more computers that are able to exchange information.
• The computer may be connected via any data
communication link, like copper wires, radio links, etc.
• They may be personal computers or large main frames.

Introduction
• The computer network may be located in a room,

• Computer networks have opened up an entire frontier
in the world of computing called the client/server model.

protocols used for connecting.

LAN
• LAN is a computer network widely used for local
communication.
• LAN connects computers in a small area like a room,
building, office, or a campus spread up to a few kilometers.
• They are privately owned networks, to exchange
information.

LAN

networking topologies.
• LAN runs at a speed of 10 mbps to 100 mbps and has
low delays.
• A LAN based on wifi wireless network technology is
called wireless local area network(WLAN).

WAN
• WAN is a network that connects computers over long
distances like cities, countries, continents or world wide.
• WAN uses public, leased, or private communication
links to spread over long distances.
• WAN uses telephone lines, satellite link and radio link to
connect.

PAGE 19
WAN
• The need to be able to connect any number of
computers at any number of sites, results in WAN
technology to be different from the LAN technology.
• It is slower and less reliable than a LAN.
• INTERNET is a common example of WAN.

Topologies
• It is the physical way in which computers are interconnected.
• Five basic network structures are :


PAGE 22
Star Topology
• Devices are connected to a central computer called
HUB.
• A Star network is particularly appropriate for
organizations that require a centralized data base or a
centralized processing facility.
• For example, a star network may be used in banking
for centralized record keeping in an on-line branch office
environment.

PAGE 23
Star Topology


PAGE 24
Advantages
• It is easy to add new and remove nodes.
• A node failure does not bring let down the entire
network.
• It is easier to diagnose network problems through a
central hub.

PAGE 25
Disadvantages
• If the central hub fails, the whole network ceases to
function.
• It costs more to cable a star configuration than other
topologies because more cable is required than other
topologies.

PAGE 26
Bus Topology
• In Bus topology a single network cable runs in the
building or campus and all nodes are linked along with
this communication line with two endpoints called the
bus or backbone.
• This structure is very popular for local area networks

PAGE 27
Bus Topology


PAGE 28
Advantages
• Reliable in very small networks as well as easy to use
and understand.
• Requires the least amount of cable to connect the
computers together and therefore is less expensive than
other cabling arrangements.

PAGE 29
Disadvantages
• Heavy network traffic can slow a bus considerably.
• Each connection between cables weakens the
electrical signal.

PAGE 30
Ring Topology
• In Ring topology the network cable passes from one
node to another until all nodes are connected in the form
of a “loop or ring”.
• Transmits in only one direction.
• Used in LAN’S and WAN’S.

PAGE 31
Ring Topology


PAGE 32
Advantages
• Ring networks can span longer distances than other
types of networks.
• Ring networks are easily extendable.

PAGE 33
Disadvantages
• Relatively expensive and difficult to install.
• Failure of one computer on the network can affect the
whole network.
• Adding or removing computers can disrupt the
network.

PAGE 34
Mesh Topology
• In mesh network, there is random connection of nodes
using communication links.
• Mesh topology is the general topology for wide area
network.
• A mesh network may be fully connected or connected
with only partial links.

PAGE 35
Mesh Topology


PAGE 36
Advantages
• The reliability is very high as there are always alternate
paths available if direct link between two nodes is down.
• Yields the greatest amount of redundancy in the event
that one of the nodes fails where network traffic can be
redirected to another node.

PAGE 37
Disadvantages
• The cost of installation and maintenance is high ( more
cable is required than any other configuration).

PAGE 38
Tree Topology
• A tree topology connects one star network to the other
star network.
• It is an extension of star topology.
• Here, we divided the whole network into segment
which can be easily managed and maintained.

PAGE 39
Tree Topology


PAGE 40
Advantages
• Each segment is provided with dedicated point-to-point
wiring to central hub.
• Error detection and correction is easy.
• If one segment is damaged, other segment are not
affected.
• Expansion of network is possible and easy.

PAGE 41
Disadvantages
• As multiple segments are connected to a central hub,
the networks depend heavily on the hub. Its failure
affects the entire network.
• Maintenance is not easy and cost are high.
• With increase in size beyond a point, the management
becomes difficult.
